Personal data requests become difficult when customer information is spread across email, spreadsheets, CRM tools, finance systems, support platforms, and backups. A written policy alone does not tell a team where to search or who is responsible for the response.

## Start with a clear intake process

Use one documented channel for requests and define who monitors it. The process should include identity verification before personal data is accessed, changed, or disclosed. Record when the request arrived, who reviewed it, and every action taken.

## Know where personal data lives

A useful data inventory connects each category of personal data to the system that stores it, the responsible owner, its purpose, and its retention period. Without that map, teams lose time asking different departments whether they hold information about the requester.

## Assign operational owners

Privacy requests often require coordinated work across legal, privacy, IT, security, customer support, and business teams. Define one accountable coordinator and named system owners before a request arrives. Each owner should know how to search, export, correct, restrict, or delete relevant records safely.

## Test the workflow

Run a tabletop exercise using a fictional customer. Track how long it takes to verify the request, locate records across systems, review exceptions, complete the requested action, and prepare a response. The test will expose unclear ownership and inaccessible data before a real deadline creates pressure.

## Keep evidence of each decision

Maintain an audit trail of the request, identity checks, systems searched, decisions made, approvals obtained, actions completed, and communications sent. This evidence helps the organization demonstrate that its process is controlled and repeatable.
