Indonesia's Personal Data Protection Law does not create a blanket rule that every company must store all data inside Indonesia. It does, however, make organizations accountable for how personal data is protected—including when it crosses national borders.

## What UU PDP actually requires

Article 56 of Law No. 27 of 2022 permits a controller to transfer personal data outside Indonesia. The controller must first ensure the destination provides an equal or higher level of protection. If that condition is not met, the controller must establish adequate and binding safeguards. If neither condition can be satisfied, consent from the data subject is required.

## Why local data infrastructure can still be the better decision

- **Simpler transfer governance:** fewer international data flows to map, assess, and document.
- **Lower latency:** applications serving Indonesian users can respond faster when workloads are closer.
- **Operational resilience:** local backup or disaster-recovery capability reduces dependency on one region.
- **Audit readiness:** data location, access, retention, and recovery evidence can be easier to explain.

## Local hosting is not the same as compliance

A Jakarta server does not automatically make an organization compliant. Weak access control, excessive retention, missing consent records, untested backups, and poor incident response remain serious risks regardless of location.

## A practical decision framework

1. Inventory personal and sensitive data.
2. Map every storage, processing, backup, and support location.
3. Identify cross-border transfers and subprocessors.
4. Check UU PDP safeguards and applicable sector regulations.
5. Define recovery objectives and test restore procedures.
6. Document why each workload is hosted locally, overseas, or in a hybrid model.

> This article provides general information, not legal advice. Regulatory requirements vary by industry and may change.
